## 3. Cultural Do's

- **Greeting:** firm but brief handshake; if Japanese counterpart bows, return with a slight nod (do not match deeply; brief sincere nod conveys respect without performative).
- **Business cards:** receive with both hands; place card on the table during the meeting (do not pocket immediately); reciprocate with HE's card presented with both hands.
- **Use full title and family name** on first reference; "Minister NAME" or "Excellency" thereafter.
- **Allow the host to lead** seating, opening remarks, and meal initiation.
- **Acknowledge Japan's craftsmanship, civilizational depth, and recent achievements** in opening remarks — Japanese counterparts respond well to a substantive, well-researched compliment.
- **Punctuality is paramount.** Arriving 5 minutes early at every venue is the norm.
- **Silence in conversation is normal.** Do not rush to fill pauses — Japanese counterparts use silence purposefully.

## 4. Cultural Don'ts

- **No pointing with the index finger**, especially during photo ops; use open hand.
- **Do not gesture with the left hand** for receiving/giving items.
- **Do not show the sole of the shoe** in seated positions.
- **Do not interrupt** — Japanese counterparts speak with measured cadence; let sentences complete.
- **Do not over-handshake** (multiple pumps reads as overly familiar).
- **No public physical contact** beyond handshake.
- **Do not photograph during prayer** (ours or theirs); do not interrupt prayer/ritual.
- **No jokes about religion, royalty, gender, or domestic politics** — even self-deprecating; even if translation seems to land, it likely didn't.
- **Do not be first to leave a meal** unless schedule dictates; pre-prepare graceful exit lines.

## 5. Japan-Specific Notes

- **Business card protocol:** Japanese counterparts will present cards with two hands, often with a slight bow. Match.
- **Bowing:** the bow is genuine but not deep for ministerial-level encounters; a slight nod with eye contact is appropriate from HE.
- **Gift-opening:** Japanese counterparts often will NOT open gifts in front of the giver. Do not press them to.
- **Color symbolism:** white and pure black in gift wrapping have funeral associations; KSA gifts pre-wrapped in green / gold / deep blue / red Saudi-courtesy wrap — avoid plain white.
- **Number symbolism:** avoid 4 and 9 in gift quantities (4 = "shi" / death, 9 = "ku" / suffering). Our dates boxes are sold in 8s or 10s.
- **Touch conventions:** minimal physical contact beyond handshake; no back-patting.
- **Speech timing:** keynotes punctually start and end on time — Japanese audiences value the discipline.
