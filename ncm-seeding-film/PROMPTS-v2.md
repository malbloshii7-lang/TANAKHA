# NCM Cloud Seeding Film — v2 prompt pack

Built to the revised brief. Fourteen shots, following the numbered sequence exactly
(items 5, 7 and 8 each break into their sub-shots).

**Model settings:** `kling3_0`, `mode: pro`, `duration: 10`, `aspect_ratio: 16:9`, `sound: off`.
Cost is 17.5 credits per shot — **245 credits for the full pack**.

## House style prefix

Prepend to every shot so the fourteen renders read as one continuous film:

> High-end stylized 3D cartoon, Thunder aesthetic: clean cel-shaded surfaces, sharp illustrated
> lines, soft volumetric lighting, calm institutional tone. Professional, accurate, restrained.
> No exaggeration, no cartoon physics.

## Flare clause (shots 7a–7c only)

The seeding hardware is the part most likely to render wrong, so it gets its own fixed block —
positive spec first, then the negatives. Use it verbatim:

> Under the wing sit neat compact racks of small cylindrical hygroscopic seeding flares, mounted
> flush against the rack and pointing REARWARD toward the tail, their red end covers facing the
> tail. The flares ignite one by one, each showing only a small controlled burn, releasing a steady
> white-grey smoke plume that trails backward and is drawn upward into the cloud base. Small
> scientific hardware only: no rocket pods, no forward-pointing tubes, no large flames, no fire.

## Shots

**01 — NCM Headquarters.** Exterior establishing shot, slow forward push-in: the modern
glass-and-steel National Center of Meteorology headquarters in Shawamekh, Abu Dhabi, under warm
late-afternoon light. The NCM logo reads clearly on the facade. White satellite dishes and
spherical radomes on the roof catch the low sun. Palm trees, still air, long soft shadows across
the plaza.

**02 — Central Forecast Office.** Interior. Forecasters in Emirati national dress — white kandura
and ghutra — at glowing consoles before a large curved radar wall showing orange and red weather
cells massing over Al Ain. One forecaster studies the display, then turns and gives the order with
quiet certainty. Slow lateral dolly, shallow depth of field, soft blue screen light.

**03 — The order travels.** Medium close-up: a female officer in a black abaya speaks calmly into
a radio handset. The monitor beside her shows, in clear legible text:
`Al Ain Airport – Seeding Flight Preparation – STATUS: READY`. Cool screen light on her face, quiet
operations-room atmosphere, camera settles and holds.

**04 — Apron 5.** Low three-quarter tracking shot at Al Ain International Airport: a white
Beechcraft King Air C90 in correct National Center of Meteorology livery begins a slow taxi across
the apron toward the runway, propellers turning, following the yellow painted taxiway centerline
markings. Desert heat haze, low golden sun, hangars behind. Measured professional patience.

**05a — Takeoff roll and rotation.** Exterior three-quarter view alongside the runway: the King Air
accelerates, nose wheel lifts, the aircraft rotates and leaves the ground. Camera pans and tracks
smoothly with it, runway markings and desert blurring past in warm late light. Realistic
aerodynamics and weight.

**05b — Cockpit rotation.** Realistic King Air cockpit, over the captain's shoulder: hands on the
control yoke ease back; through the windshield the runway centerline slides away and drops beneath
the nose as the horizon rises. Correct King Air panel layout, instruments glowing softly, warm
light across the coaming. Steady restrained camera.

**05c — Climb-out.** Rear three-quarter air-to-air: the King Air climbs away from Al Ain, main
landing gear folding up into the engine nacelles, then banking with visible aileron and rudder
deflection. Living aerodynamic flight, never sliding. Desert city and airport shrinking below in
hazy golden air.

**06 — Long aerial cruise.** Held air-to-air shot, almost still: the King Air flies steadily at
altitude in soft late light toward the long ridged silhouette of Jebel Hafeet rising from the
desert, and on toward the higher folds of the Hajar mountains layered in atmospheric haze. Give
the sky time — this is the longest, quietest shot in the film.

**07a — Under the cells.** The King Air flies level beneath realistic convective cloud cells,
tall grey-white towers with dark flat bases and visible internal texture. Wide, patient framing,
the aircraft small against the weather. Soft god rays through the gaps.

**07b — The release.** Cockpit interior close shot: cloud fills the windshield as the aircraft
levels. The pilot's hand moves deliberately to the flare release button on the console and presses
it; a small indicator light illuminates. Cloud-filtered light across the panel. Calm precision.

**07c — The flares.** View from a passenger cabin window looking out at the wing.
*(Insert the flare clause above verbatim.)* Intimate, exact framing — the racks fill the lower
frame, the cloud base fills the upper.

**08a — Final approach.** Exterior three-quarter: the King Air descends toward the Al Ain runway
beneath the dark rain-bearing clouds it has seeded, distant curtains of light rain over the desert.
Gear down, flaps extended, approach lights ahead. Realistic weight and glide path.

**08b — Touchdown.** Cockpit view through the windshield as the runway rises to meet the aircraft;
the main wheels touch with a small honest puff of tyre smoke, centerline markings streaming past,
nose settling. Restrained camera, no shake theatrics.

**08c — Return to rest.** The King Air taxis slowly back to Apron 5 under rain-washed light,
turns onto the stand and stops. Propellers wind down. Quiet, still, calm final frame.

## Changes from v1

- Sequence expanded from 12 to 14 shots: takeoff (05) and landing (08) now render as three
  distinct shots each, as the brief specifies, rather than being compressed.
- New shot 07a (aircraft under convective cells) and 08b (cockpit touchdown), neither of which
  existed in v1.
- The flare hardware is now a fixed reusable clause with explicit orientation — rearward-pointing,
  red covers facing the tail — and the negatives are stated as hardware facts rather than mood
  notes. v1 said only "no flames, no rockets"; it did not specify orientation, which is the detail
  most likely to render wrong.
- Shot 01 now calls out the NCM logo explicitly; shot 05b calls out correct King Air panel layout.
